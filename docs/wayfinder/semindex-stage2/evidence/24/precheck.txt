uniformly.  The pooled ratio is the quantity; the mean of the 20 per-run ratios

decision of a run is ever in the fit and the read-out at once.  Targets, all in

the same pair, truncated at the end of the pair's chain.  K = 1 is the one-period
target.  rho is the cell's pooled rho from part 2.

Three predictors, each fitted on the four fit folds and read out on the fifth:
  cell mean    the mean of y over the fit rows of the same (cell, interval): 15

==============================================================================
4  THE SAME QUESTION AS A RANKING, WHICH IS ALL A DECIDER NEEDS
==============================================================================
A decider does not need the cost of an interval, only the ORDER of the three.  So:
on the read-out rows, give the ridge all three intervals' predictions of the
one-period target and take its argmin.  The logged interval happens to equal that
argmin on some rows; compare the realised y of THOSE rows with the realised y of
all read-out rows.  y is a cost, so a negative difference is the direction a
decider would want.

WHAT THIS IS.  A one-period, logged-policy-continuation reading.  It says what
followed the subset of logged decisions the predictor would have agreed with,
while every decision after them was still drawn uniformly.  It says NOTHING about
the replay score of a policy that took the argmin at every decision: that
policy's own continuation is itself, and no replay is run here.  The standard
error is clustered on runs -- the 20 per-run differences, SE = sd / sqrt(20) --
because two decisions of one run share a record stream and are not independent.

The mean of y over ALL read-out rows of a cell is 0 to rounding, because rho is
that cell's own rate and y is centred on it (part 2), so the `all` column below
is a computed 0 and not a typed one.

  p     matched rows   share   mean y matched   mean y all   difference      SE
  0.2            1046  0.3425           -81.72         0.00       -82.45    9.57
  0.4            1016  0.3332          -118.56         0.00      -117.39    9.71

The control that says whether the ORDER carries anything: the same realised y by
the RANK the ridge gave the interval the log happened to draw.  Rank 0 is the
subset above.

WHY THIS SEPARATES THE INTERVAL FROM THE ROW.  The logging policy drew the
interval uniformly and independently of the state, so a row falls into rank 0,
1 or 2 with probability one third whatever its features are: the three rank
groups are three samples of the SAME state distribution, which is why their
counts come out near equal below.  A difference in realised y between rank 0 and
rank 2 is therefore the interval and not the row.  Had the predictor only been
finding cheap ROWS, all three ranks would hold the same mixture of cheap and dear
rows and the three means would agree.

==============================================================================
5  THE PERMUTED-TARGET CONTROL
==============================================================================
The same leave-seeds-out fit with the FIT folds' targets permuted among their own
rows, so every input keeps its value and loses its target.  The permutation uses
numpy.random.default_rng(20261125), a fixed seed, so this part reproduces.

WHAT IT SHOWS: that part 3's R squared is not an artefact of the arithmetic.  The
only thing the permutation removes is the link between a fit row's inputs and its
own target, and the prediction then explains nothing held out, so no part of part
3's number came from the read-out targets reaching the fit through the code.  The
negative value is also a reading of how much a 261-feature ridge over-fits four
folds of these rows when there is nothing to find.

WHAT IT DOES NOT SHOW: that no cell of the vector carries a leaked future value.
Such a cell would lose its link under the permutation too, so a legitimate input
and a leaked one collapse alike here.  What rules a leak out is structural:
observe.observation builds the record from the state, the resolution and the
history as they stand at the decision's own ts_ms and from nothing else, and part
3's coefficient table names the cells the fit leans on.  Nor does it detect a
read-out row left in the fit: that row would carry a PERMUTED target there and
could not be memorised.  check_held_out is what detects that, and part 6 shows it
firing.

==============================================================================
8  WHAT THIS DOES NOT SHOW
==============================================================================
- No policy and no score.  Nothing here is a decider, no replay simulation is
  run, and the per-decision agreement between the emulator and the replay is
  recorded as failed in evidence/13.  Every number is a property of the logs.
- Part 3's R squared is explanation, not effect.  The continuation after every
  row is the logging policy's, and a decision's cost at the downstream pair is
  charged to that pair and appears in no target here.
- rho is estimated on all 20 seeds of its cell and then used in the target of the
  read-out rows too, so one scalar of out-of-fold information is in those
  targets.  Part 3's fit-fold-rho table is that bound as a measurement: refitting
  with rho taken from each fold's fit rows alone moves no held-out R squared in
  the table by more than 0.0022.
- The penalty 10.0 is fixed and not tuned.  A penalty chosen against these folds
  would make them part of the fit; nothing here says it is the best penalty.
- Part 4 is one period deep and reads the logged policy's own continuation.  It
  is not a replay score and it is not any policy's value.
- Nothing about an interval finer than the three, which the logs do not carry,
  and nothing about any seed above 20 or about the label rows.
- The 320 decisions that own no period (one per pair per run) are in no table
  above; their cost is inside part 1's closing identity and nowhere else.
- No standard error on any pooled rate: part 2's SE is over runs and part 4's is
  clustered on runs, and neither is a confidence statement about a policy.

no check fired on real input; the failures in part 6 are the mutations
