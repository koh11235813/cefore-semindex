THIS IS NOT M.

M is the decision maker learned from the s11a EMULATOR logs and scored in the
replay.  This file touches neither.  It fits a small classifier to ticket 09's
LABEL rows and reports its agreement with those labels on seeds 71 to 80.
A label is the arm that is best IF the formula rule takes over afterwards,
measured inside the replay over 8 passes, so agreement with it is a measure of
neither arm and of no policy.  Nothing below is M's number, nothing below is a
score, and no replay simulation and no emulator log is read here at all.
The question it answers is narrower: is there learnable signal in these labels?

==============================================================================
2  THE THREE PREDICTORS
==============================================================================
All three are fitted on seeds 1 to 70 of their own row set and read out on 71 to
80.  No read-out row's seed is in any fit (asserted in part 1).

  full vector  closed-form ridge from the 261-cell feature vector to the three
               one-hot targets, penalty 10.0 on features standardised on the fit
               rows, the answer being the largest of the three outputs
  five cells   the same arithmetic on the five scalar-cell indicators alone,
               which is what ADR 0004 line 5 permits a policy to read
  majority     always the fit rows' most frequent arm, which reads no input

The feature vector is built from each row's own observation through
features.record_from_label_row, which is observe.observation on the row's
`state`, `resolved`, `history`, `ts_ms` and `neighbor` -- the same five fields,
in the same call, that the language model's text was rendered from.  A tie among
the three outputs goes to the first arm in the order
[3.695, 6.0, 10.043], which is the rule the language model's three words
were picked by.

3  THE READ-OUT, SEEDS 71 TO 80
==============================================================================
Agreement is the share of read-out rows whose answer is the row's label.  The
baseline is the always-10.043 s answer on THOSE rows, which is also the most
frequent arm there.  The difference is paired inside a bootstrap of 2000 resamples
of the TEN read-out seeds with replacement (numpy.random.default_rng(20261125)),
so the SE carries the between-run spread and not a between-row one.  The `diff`
column is that bootstrap's own mean of the paired difference, which sits a few
ten-thousandths off the plain difference because a resample reweights the seeds;
the plain difference is the one the verdict below the table divides by the SE.

The language model's own agreement on the SAME rows is the last column, read off
../23/tune.txt's own part 4: its kept epoch's held-out agreement.  It is the
number this module exists to stand beside.

==============================================================================
7  WHAT THIS RESULT CAN AND CANNOT MEAN
==============================================================================
- A LABEL IS NOT A VERDICT ON AN ARM.  It is the arm that is best IF the formula
  rule takes over afterwards, measured inside the replay over 8 passes.  So a
  high agreement means a classifier matched that quantity, not that it would
  lower a score, and a low one does not say the arm was wrong.
- THE LABELS DISAGREE WITH THEMSELVES.  Two independent 8-pass labellings of the
  same decisions agreed on 0.7577 of the 1366 decisions they both labelled
  (../22/stability.txt, seeds 1 to 10 of this cell, read off that file and not
  typed here).  So 0.2423 of them got a DIFFERENT label, and a classifier that
  matched these labels perfectly would be matching a teacher that disagrees with
  itself about a quarter of the time.
  That share is an upper bound on the labels that are pass noise, not an
  estimate of it: one disagreement needs only one of the two sides to be wrong.
- AN ARGMAX AND A SIGNAL ARE NOT THE SAME QUESTION.  The five cells' argmax equals
  the prior exactly, and their Brier score and the cross-tab both say they carry
  label information.  So nothing here says the ADR 0004 scalars carry no signal;
  what it says is that a three-arm argmax read-out of them cannot express the
  signal they have, because 10.043 is the majority inside all five cells.  How
  much of the full vector's own gain lies OUTSIDE the cells is not decomposed
  here: its Brier gain is the larger of the two on both row sets, and no number
  in this file splits it into a part the cells reach and a part they do not.
- AGREEMENT IS NOT A SCORE.  No replay simulation is run here and no score is
  computed.  The choice between any two deciders is made on replay scores of the
  selection seeds, which this file does not read.
- THE ROW SETS ARE NOT A SAMPLE OF DECISIONS.  Candidate a drops the ties and
  candidate b keeps about four tenths of a, selected by the paired t of the very
  passes that produced the label, so b's rows are the ones the labelling was
  most sure of and its baseline is higher for that reason.
- NOTHING HERE IS ABOUT M.  M reads emulator logs, not label rows, and the rows
  it learns from carry no label at all: the labels were attached to decisions
  inside a replay roll-in, so there is no row-level join between the two.
- Nothing about the p 0.4 label cell, which is not read, and nothing about seeds
  81 to 100, which are the selection half.
- The penalty 10.0 is fixed and not tuned; nothing here says it is the best one,
  and a penalty chosen against the read-out rows would make them part of the fit.
