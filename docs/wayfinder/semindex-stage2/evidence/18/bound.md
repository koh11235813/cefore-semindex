Generated by `bound.py run`, 12 workers, wall clock 247 s (arms and the evidence/15 rules 51 s, F search and scoring 165 s, V bisection and final 23 s).

Everything below is **simulated against simulated**. No number of this step has a testbed measurement behind it. ### Why most budget targets were missed

Added after the run, as a diagnostic of the step's own method. It changes no number above and no target entered or left the reading rule because of it. Every missed target falls into one of two classes:

- **(a) out of V's reach at any price.** V's poll count at the floor of the price bracket, 2 ms per poll, is already below the target on at least one fold. The count falls as the price rises, so on that fold no price in the bracket reaches the target and the bisection had nothing to find. The price is bisected per fold, so one such fold is enough to miss the target. The class column names the folds where this happened when the other fold could reach the target.

- **(b) inside V's reach, stepped over by the staircase.** The floor count is at or above the target, and the bisection landed outside the 2 % tolerance anyway, because the poll count is a staircase in the price whose tread around that target is narrower than eight bisection steps can resolve.

### What V knows

Stated from the code, so that no reader takes V for a policy. V's schedule is solved from three things a node running the protocol does not have:

- **Every record's creation instant.** `solve_v_run` hands the DP of hop 1 the creation instants of the workload itself, so V knows at hop 1 exactly when each record came into existence at the origin.

- **The whole link timeline, in advance.** `feasible_instant` and `candidate_set` read every link_down and every link_up of the pair before placing an instant, so V never puts a poll into an outage and knows the first millisecond after each outage ends.

- **The availability instant of every record at every upstream hop.** Hop h's candidate set and objective are built from `stored_at` of the hop above, that is from the realised instants at which the upstream receiver actually held each record under V's own schedule. So the polls of successive hops are chained: hop h+1 is told when hop h will hold the record and places its poll just after that. A record's discovery wait is then paid about once along the path, where a node that polls on an interval pays a wait at every hop.

## Deviations from spec.md

1. **The bootstrap does not redo the coordinate ascent inside each resample.** spec.md asks for the arms, the selected members and the pooled value to be recomputed per resample. The arms and the pooled value are. The selection is not: 2000 coordinate ascents per cell and workload would be about 300 million simulations, against the 166000 the whole step costs. Each seed's value is still an out-of-sample one, so the interval carries the variance of the scoring half but not the variance of the selection itself.
2. **Every gain of this step is pooled from per-seed means**, on the early-record scoring and on the all-record one alike, through ../13/trickle.py's `seed_matrix`. evidence/15 pools all 100 runs for its all-record point estimate. For sums the two orders agree exactly; for the ratios they differ in the last digits.
3. **The DP charges a record no poll reaches at t0+130 s**, the last instant a poll could be sent. spec.md fixes the objective but not the tail, and without a finite tail penalty the DP drops the end of the run for free.
4. **A timeout leaves the consecutive-empty counter where it was**, and a non-empty reply resets it. spec.md says `k_cap` counts consecutive empty replies and does not say what a timeout does to that count.
5. **The bracket ends are not evaluated, and the last two of the eight steps interpolate.** The poll count is monotone in the price, so an unevaluated end costs nothing and buys two more steps inside the bracket. Even so the tolerance was met on few targets; the counts are under the V table, the price ladder says why, and the consequence is that the reading rule has almost nothing to read.
6. **`V_at_F`'s budget is the selected member's own poll count on the fold it is scored on**, one per fold, rather than F's pooled 20-seed count. spec.md adds "F's pooled realised polls per run" to the bisection targets and also says that when the two folds select members with different poll counts both are tried. The per-fold form is what makes `V_at_F` pair with F seed by seed, because each seed is then scored against V at the budget of the member F used on that seed.
7. **"Every V target was dropped" is not one of spec.md's four named reasons for `undecided`.** spec.md lists four: V_max straddles H, V_max straddles `H - 6`, `V_at_F` does not exist, and V is not defined under WV. A cell where every target of the reachable set missed its budget is a fifth case, and it is the one most cells of this run fall into. It is reported under `undecided` by name.
8. **The post-timeout floor is reported per cell, workload and target**, not per pair. spec.md asks for it per pair. It bound no transition anywhere, so the per-pair breakdown would be a table of zeroes.
9. **`bound_v.csv` holds replicate 0 only.** All five replicates are solved and scored; writing every one of them would be 16800 timetable rows, and one replicate is what a fixed-timetable testbed run would take.
10. **The reachability cap is counted as the scratchpad counted it**: per directed pair, the distinct creation instants of the origin whose chain it carries plus the link_up instants of that pair. That is the count spec.md's own table is stated against, and this step reproduces that table exactly. The DP's own candidate set differs from it above hop 1, where it uses the realised availability at the holder and pushes every instant forward to a feasible one.
11. **A V target is the 20-seed cell value of the target's realised polls per run**, while the bisection measures V's polls on its own five bisection seeds. spec.md names the target as a cell-level number and runs the bisection on five seeds, so the two are measured on different seed sets and part of the residual gap is seed variation rather than a failure to bisect.
12. **`i_timeout` and `k_cap` live in the rule dict as keys.** A member with `i_timeout` keep and `k_cap` None carries both keys and still runs through the base classes alone, which is what checks 1 and 2 measure.
13. **Members with `i_max` below `i_min` are skipped.** spec.md's table gives `i_max` the arms' intervals at or above `i_min`, so a sweep of `i_min` past the current `i_max` would leave the family; those candidates are not evaluated.
14. **F_reach is taken from the members the restricted search already evaluated.** spec.md says it is computed in the same search; a search of its own over the reachable sub-family would be a second ascent and a second search path. One consequence is that F_reach can be missing where spec.md's addendum expects it: the members the restricted ascent visited on one fold can all sit above the reachability cap, and then that fold selects nothing and F_reach is not scored. The F table names the cell where this happened and by how much the fold's best visited member missed the cap.
15. **Check 4's gate excuses a realised poll far from every chosen instant when the clamp or the provisional timer moved at least as many polls** (`far > clamped + dropped` is what fails the gate). spec.md states the within-100 ms property with no such clause, so a run in which the clamp or the timer moved polls could pass the gate with realised polls far from every chosen instant. This run's own counts are printed under the check table.
