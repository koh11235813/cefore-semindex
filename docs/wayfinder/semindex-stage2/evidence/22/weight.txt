Why step 3 moved the weight, measured rather than asserted
  The question: the jitter of steps 1 and 2 comes from the node's own stream, seeded with (seed, node name) and NOT with the replicate, so all 40 streams of a seed share one jitter sequence and the jitter is averaged over 100 independent draws; steps 3 and 4 key it per (seed, replicate) and average over 4000. If that is the whole story, the +3.001866 ms is sampling noise of the step-2 measurement.
  Jackknife standard error of the weight over the 100 seeds, and the move set against it:
    step 2   weight   101.467047   jackknife se 3.167161 ms
    step 3   weight   104.468914   jackknife se 2.094254 ms
    the step 2 -> step 3 move is +3.001866 ms = 0.95 x step 2's own standard error and 1.43 x step 3's
    step 2's standard error is 1.51 times step 3's; claim 1 asks for at least 2.0
  The same seeds read twice, paired: per seed, both arms pooled over the 40 streams, step 3's value minus step 2's. The lag stream of a (seed, replicate) is seeded identically in the two, so the jitter keying is the only input that differs.

  Against the rule fixed before the measurement (claim 1: step 2's standard error is at least 2.0 times step 3's; claim 2: the paired move of the weight is within 2.0 standard errors of zero)
    claim 1 DOES NOT HOLD, claim 2 HOLDS
    UNDECIDED: the paired move is 1.17 standard errors from zero, inside the band the rule leaves open, so this measurement neither supports the hypothesis nor refutes it.
    What is left unmeasured is how the seed-to-seed variance splits. Claim 1 asks the per-pair keying to cut the standard error by a factor, and it cuts it by less, which means the jitter is not the only thing varying from seed to seed: the outage instants and the record schedule vary too and are IDENTICAL in the two steps, so they sit in both standard errors and dilute the ratio. Splitting the two would need a sweep that holds the jitter draws fixed while the lags vary, and this script makes no such run.
  Either way, and about the KEYING move alone: the step 2 -> step 3 move is 0.95 times the step-2 weight's own standard error and the paired move is 1.17 of its own, inside the 2.0 standard errors claim 2 allows, so step 2's 101.467047 and step 3's 104.468914 do not separate at these 100 seeds and this cell cannot tell the two jitter keyings apart.
  The step 1 -> step 2 move of +0.845362 ms is a different kind of quantity and no standard error covers it: it is the SAME 8000 simulations of this cell scored twice, the censored records left out of the delay and then entered at their lower bound, so not one seed-to-seed draw enters the difference and its sign is settled in advance -- only the long arm has censored records, so leaving them out shortened its delay and the committed weight was biased DOWNWARD.
  The whole distance from the committed value to the fixed one, +3.674274 ms, is 1.16 times step 2's standard error: larger than that standard error, and inside two of them. A reader who wants one sentence for it: the two values sit within two standard errors of each other, and the part of the distance that a rerun on other seeds could move is the keying move.
  The uncertainty a reader should carry on the FIXED weight: step 4 is 104.295959 ms per poll with a jackknife standard error of 1.685586 ms over the 100 seeds.
  The jackknife of steps 2 and 3 is taken on a MEDIAN over the 40 streams, so it is an approximation there and its leave-one-out values can sit at a few levels rather than spread smoothly; step 4's is a mean and has no such caveat. On this data the risk did not materialise: the 100 leave-one-out values are 100 distinct for step 2, 100 for step 3 and 100 for step 4. The paired comparison does not depend on the median at all.
