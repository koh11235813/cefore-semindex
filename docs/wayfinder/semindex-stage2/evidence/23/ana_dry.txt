
========================================================================
0  WHAT THIS IS, AND WHAT IT CANNOT BE
========================================================================
A DRY RUN.  The csv it reads is of TUNING seeds that both candidates were fitted
on, so every score, every gain and every verdict below is an exercise of the
arithmetic on training data and says NOTHING about which candidate is better.
The bootstrap is degenerate at two seeds: a resample of two positions has only
three distinct means, so the interval below is the arithmetic running and not an
interval.

This script reads one csv and nothing else: no card, no model, no run store, no
label file.  It prints no clock time and no path of the machine it ran on, so the
two hosts' output can be compared with cmp.

========================================================================
3  THE REPORTED FRONTIER GAIN, WITH ITS 95 % INTERVAL
========================================================================
A DIFFERENT DELAY FROM THE ONE ABOVE: `predict.vals` is `lat / n4`, i.e. the censored
records DROPPED, pooled over a seed's runs and then over the seeds.  The score above
selects; this is reported beside it, and the two disagree about the censored records
by construction.

the three re-run arms are stored under ../20's own frontier keys, so that interp and
gain stay single-sourced:
  const3.695   -> s10b3p695
  const6.000   -> s10b6p0
  const10.043  -> s10b10p043

OK: the per-seed row handed to predict.gain is [n4, c4, lat, polls], checked against
the four sums read out of the csv by column name, and predict.vals reads the delay,
the polls and the censored share back out of it.  The order is a silent defect and
not an error: the order [lat, n, c, polls] gives a "delay" of n4 / lat, five orders
of magnitude off, and prints a plausible gain.  Shown firing in part 5.

the frontier, as interp reads it (pooled delay against a seed's poll count, which is
2 times a run's; interp is piecewise linear in log-log, so a factor common to the
frontier's x and the decider's x cancels and the gain is the same number -- checked
below against the same rows divided by 2):
  s10b3p695      delay  13561.267 ms   polls    513.000 per seed ( 256.500 per run)   censored 0.0000
  s10b6p0        delay  23361.862 ms   polls    319.000 per seed ( 159.500 per run)   censored 0.0000
  s10b10p043     delay  30314.483 ms   polls    191.500 per seed (  95.750 per run)   censored 0.0000
  monotone (more polls, less delay): True

the resample loop is this script's own and adopts ../20's drop-and-count rule by
reference: 2000 resamples of the 2 SEEDS from one integers(0, 2, size=(2000, 2))
matrix drawn once with default_rng(20261122).  A resample whose arms are not monotone, or
whose decider's polls leave the arms, YIELDS NO GAIN AND IS COUNTED AND DROPPED, not
substituted, because substituting a value would hide how often the frontier is
unreadable; the dropped count is printed beside every interval.

  decider        gain      95 % interval            half width   dropped   word
  F             0.2230     13.306 % to   29.375 %      8.034 %         0   seen
  untuned      -0.0624    -12.260 % to   -0.708 %      5.776 %         0   reversed
  a             0.0602      4.684 % to    7.284 %      1.300 %         0   seen
  b             0.0899      4.724 % to   16.147 %      5.711 %         0   seen

The real mode's 95 % interval, at 20 seeds, is expected to be wider than ../20's at
40 by roughly sqrt(2); that is an INFERENCE and not a measurement, and the printed
half width is what counts.  This csv carries 2 seeds.
boot_gains is NOT called: its TESTED keys name ../20's two rules and not this step's
four model-or-rule deciders.

========================================================================
4  HOW THE CHOICE BETWEEN a AND b IS MADE
========================================================================
mean score      a    38018.044 ms,  b    37605.828 ms
gap             412.216 ms, the lower being b
the band        1.685586 ms per poll x 176.220 polls = 297.0340 ms, and the RULE the frozen
                plan states is 297.0 ms.  It is the jackknife standard error
                ../22/weight.txt attaches to the fixed weight, times the midpoint of
                the two outer arms' step-4 poll counts, i.e. the amount by which
                re-solving the weight on another draw of seeds would move a typical
                run's score.  IT IS NOT A CONFIDENCE INTERVAL ON THE DIFFERENCE and
                it is not the seed-to-seed spread of a mean score, which the paired
                bootstrap below carries.  The tighter of weight.txt's two readings
                was taken (the per-stream weight range gives 3.676 x 176.220 = 647.8
                ms), which makes "separated" the EASIER verdict and not the harder
                one, so the separated branch is the weaker-evidence branch of the two.

the part of the band that does NOT cancel: a weight error shifts both candidates in
the same direction and cancels to the extent their poll counts agree, so
  1.685586 x |polls(a) - polls(b)| = 1.685586 x |109.750 - 150.750| = 69.109 ms
is the part of the wobble that survives the cancellation.  It is computable only
after the scores exist, so it is reported and is not the rule.

the paired bootstrap over the same 2 seeds and the same index matrix, whose per-seed
value is the MEAN OF THAT SEED'S 2 RUN SCORES -- the only aggregate under which
resampling 2 seed indices reproduces the 4-run mean the rule compares:
  score(a) - score(b): point 412.216 ms, 95 % interval -3311.291 to 4135.722 ms, contains 0: True
  distinct resample values: 3 of 2000
  AT TWO SEEDS THIS IS DEGENERATE: a resample of two positions has three distinct
  means, so the interval is the arithmetic running and not an interval.

VERDICT: SEPARATED, NOT RESOLVED.  The gap of 412.216 ms clears the 297.0 ms band,
so the relabelling clause stays shut and the chosen candidate is the lower one,
b; but the gap lies inside the paired bootstrap's 95 % interval (-3311.291 to 4135.722
ms, which contains 0), so this report does not call the two distinguished.

THE CANDIDATE THIS ANALYSIS CARRIES FORWARD: b
...on TUNING seeds, so that name is the arithmetic's answer and not the step's.

The selection bias of choosing one of two candidates on the same seeds is not
corrected anywhere: lower is better and the rule picks the lower of two, so the
winner's figure is the MINIMUM OF TWO DRAWS and is biased in the favourable
direction.  It reaches the gain as well, which is a different delay on the same runs,
and it reaches the L-against-F and L-against-arm readings, each of which is then a
best-of-two against a single comparator.

========================================================================
6  WHAT THIS DOES NOT SHOW
========================================================================
- NOTHING about which candidate is better.  The csv is of tuning seeds that both
  candidates were fitted on; the verdict above is the arithmetic's and not the
  step's, and the bootstrap over two seeds is degenerate.
- Nothing about L on the testbed.  evidence/13's per-decision agreement is on record
  as failing, and Mode B's band is a per-stage band and not a bound on one decision.
- Nothing that corrects the selection bias of choosing one of two candidates on the
  same seeds.  The only clean correction is a set of seeds nobody has looked at.
- Nothing about the difference between a decider's score and an arm's being the
  decider's difference alone.  F and L draw one extra tick lag whenever they change
  their mind between a send and a resolution and the three fixed arms never do, so
  the comparator family consumes the pair streams differently by construction.
- Nothing about the weight being right.  Its jackknife standard error is 1.685586 ms
  per poll and the band above is built from exactly that uncertainty; the selecting
  statistic moves with it.
- Nothing about the agreement with the labels, which agree.py reports and which
  predicts no score in either direction.
- No pass or fail on any decider.  The verdict in part 4 is a choice between two
  candidates and not a judgement of L against F or against the arms.
