evidence/23 -- preparing the tuning host and proving the replay runs there identically
2026-11-24.  Nothing tuned is kept and nothing tuned is measured in this step.  Three
parts: the host, the inputs and the replay (sections 1-5), the first forward passes of the
base model on that host (6-7), and the four measurements the tuning plan of L rests on
(10-12: read-out noise, parallel throughput, the legend line, one optimiser step).

WHAT THIS STEP ESTABLISHES
  1. A venv on the tuning host that sees the RTX PRO 5000 Blackwell as sm_120 and carries
     the gemma4 architecture.
  2. The base weights of google/gemma-4-E2B-it on that host, as plain files, pinned to one
     commit, with the content digest matching the hub's own LFS object id.
  3. The replay's inputs there: the hindsight labels, the 160 run files of the main cell,
     and the code at the relative paths two scripts on the replay path assert.
  4. That one port simulation produces the SAME BYTES on both machines, for six seeds and
     four deciders, although the two interpreters are python 3.12.13 and 3.13.5.
  5. That the checkpoint loads in bf16 on that card as Gemma4ForConditionalGeneration,
     that the chat template leaves the prefilled answer prefix as the last thing in the
     prompt, that " short" / " mid" / " long" each add exactly one token THERE, and that
     the three logits read out of one forward pass repeat bit for bit.
  6. What one pass costs on this card: 43.8 ms at batch 1, and 152.4 passes a second at
     batch 8, so 128 000 passes take about 14 minutes.
  7. WHAT BATCHING COSTS IN DECISIONS, which is the number item 6 does not give: in bf16,
     a batch of 8 changes the argmax of 14 of 512 label rows against a batch-1 pass of the
     same row.  In float32 it changes none.
  8. That a SECOND PROCESS, running alone beside an idle parent, repeats all 512 x 3
     bf16 batch-1 logits bit for bit.  Whether K concurrent processes do is untested:
     part 2 timed them and never compared their logits.
  9. What an optimiser step costs: 0.26 s at batch 8 and 0.45 s at batch 16, so one epoch
     over the 11 438 labelled rows of seeds 1-80 is 5 to 6 minutes.

WHAT IT DOES NOT ESTABLISH
  Nothing about L as a decider.  No label-agreement number, no replay score for any tuned
  model, and no decider in this step asks a model anything.  Section 11 DOES attach a
  LoRA and take 61 optimiser steps, but only to time them: the adapter was never written
  to disk, it was deleted with the process, and no tuned model was read out on the
  selection seeds.  Deciding anything from those 61 steps would be deciding before the
  plan is frozen, which is what this step exists to avoid.  The model read out in
  sections 6 and 11 is UNTUNED or barely moved, so which of the three words wins a given
  prompt is not evidence of anything; only the mechanics and the costs are.  The four
  deciders of the parity check are the three constant arms and the formula rule F, i.e.
  the whole comparison except the candidate.  A forward pass on a GPU is not
  bit-reproducible across machines in general and nothing here claims it is: sections 6
  and 11 show repeatability on ONE machine, in two processes of it, one at a time.
