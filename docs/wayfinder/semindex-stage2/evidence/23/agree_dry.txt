CANDIDATE a's EPOCH IS NOT DISTINGUISHED BY ANYTHING IN THIS STEP, and that is said
here rather than left to a reader to divide two numbers.  A ratio is printed above
only where this run reads out the very rows the margin was taken on, because a ratio
of two numbers from two populations reads like evidence and is not; and where it is
printed, the standard error is the LARGER of the two, so the margin is a fraction of
the uncertainty on one epoch's own mean rather than a multiple of it.  The quantity
that would settle it is the standard error of the PAIRED per-row cross-entropy
differences between two epochs, which is smaller than the unpaired figure above and
is NOT computable any more: the keep rule deleted the two epochs that were not kept,
so no per-row cross-entropy of epoch 1 or epoch 2 exists on either machine.  So a's
kept epoch is reported as arbitrary among its three, and the number above is its
scale and not its test.

========================================================================
5  WHAT THIS RUN DOES NOT SHOW
========================================================================
- NOTHING about which candidate is better, and nothing the ticket asks for.  These
  are seeds 71 to 80, the rows that chose each candidate's kept epoch, so they
  are neither the selection half nor a held-out slice in the ordinary sense.
- Nothing about a score.  The choice between the candidates is the mean replay
  score's and never this number's, and an agreement near the labels and an agreement
  far from them are equally uninformative about that score: the states these labels
  sit on came from a uniformly random roll-in, the label is the best arm under an F
  suffix L does not supply, and in the replay L drives the run and reads its own
  choices back in the history column.
- Nothing about accuracy against testbed truth.  The labels are the replay's own
  hindsight and two independent 8-pass labellings of the same decisions agreed on
  0.7577 of them, with the 0.90 bar fixed in advance never reached.
- Nothing about the state being read rather than the clock.  The rendered text
  carries the history length and the ages of the last exchanges, so the position in
  the run is readable from the input, and the labels' own reliability moves with that
  position (0.5101 in a run's first quarter against 0.9597 in its last).
- Nothing about a class prior being learnt instead of the state.  The baseline above
  is what always answering the most frequent arm already buys.
- Nothing about the pass time.  The ms per row above includes the render and the
  tokenizer call and is not a latency measurement; latency.py is.
