
========================================================================
0  WHAT THIS IS, AND WHAT IT IS NOT
========================================================================
THIS IS NOT IN THE FROZEN PLAN.  spec.md carries this measurement in one sentence,
amendment A7's last: scoring the first tuning run's two adapters on the same seeds
and streams is "not part of this plan and is run only if the user asks for it".
The user asked, the run was made, and this is its reading.
IT WAS WRITTEN AFTER EVERY SCORE OF BOTH RUNS WAS SEEN.
IT SELECTS NOTHING.  The candidates are the SECOND run's two adapters, by amendment
A3, which was written and committed before either run was scored: "the second run's
adapters are the candidates whatever its numbers are".  The first run's b scores
lower than the second run's b below; that is a number to report and not a choice to
make, because choosing between the two runs' adapters on the seeds they were both
scored on is the one thing A3 fixed against.  ana_run1.txt's line "THE CANDIDATE
THIS ANALYSIS CARRIES FORWARD: b" is a line ana.py prints for any csv it is handed;
on the first run's csv it carries nothing forward.
n = 2 TUNING RUNS IS TWO POINTS AND NOT A DISTRIBUTION.  The two run-to-run rows
below are a difference of two draws of the recipe, not an estimate of its spread.
EVERY INTERVAL IS OVER THE 20 SEEDS WITH THE TWO ADAPTERS HELD FIXED.  So it says
how far the seeds let a difference between two particular weight files move, and it
says nothing about a third run of the recipe.

It reads two csvs and nothing else: no card, no model, no run store, no label file,
no ROOT.  It prints no clock time and no path, so two runs of it are byte-identical.

========================================================================
4  THE FRONTIER GAIN, WHICH THIS SCRIPT DOES NOT RECOMPUTE
========================================================================
The gains of the four adapters are in ana.txt's part 3 and ana_run1.txt's part 3,
each with its 95 % interval, its half width, its dropped count and its word, and
THEY ARE LEFT THERE.  Recomputing them here would need ana.py's per-seed
[n4, c4, lat, polls] row builder, its arm-name-to-frontier-key mapping, its two
row-order checks, its scale-invariance check and its drop-and-count resample loop
around predict.gain -- not a few lines, and a second route to a number the archives
already print would be a second place for that number to be wrong.  The two archives
are ana.py's own output on the two csvs, so reading the four gains off them side by
side needs no code at all.

========================================================================
6  WHAT THIS DOES NOT SHOW
========================================================================
- NO CHOICE OF ANY KIND.  The candidates are the second run's adapters by A3, fixed
  before either run was scored, and the first run's lower b does not reopen that.
  The rule A3 protects is exactly the one a reader of this table would break by
  picking the better of two runs on the seeds both were scored on.
- NOTHING ABOUT A THIRD RUN OF THE RECIPE.  Two runs are two points: there is no
  spread of the recipe here with a degree of freedom to spare, and the intervals
  above hold the two weight files fixed and resample only the seeds.
- Nothing about WHERE the two runs parted.  Neither run asked torch for a
  deterministic kernel, which is the recipe as frozen, and no log of either run
  carries a digest of the untuned adapter, so the seeded lora_A draw is unverified
  as well.  This script reads two csvs of scores and reaches none of that.
- Nothing that corrects the selection bias.  The chosen candidate's figure is still
  the minimum of two draws on these seeds, and the only clean correction is a set of
  seeds nobody has looked at.  Four adapters on the same 20 seeds is more reads of
  those seeds, not fewer.
- No correction for multiplicity, and the pairs were chosen after every mean was on
  the screen.  Twelve differences are printed here beside versus.txt's nine.
- Nothing about a difference against a comparator being the decider's difference
  alone.  F and L draw one extra tick lag whenever they change their mind between a
  send and a resolution and the three fixed arms never do, so the comparator family
  consumes the pair streams differently by construction, in an unknown direction.
- Nothing about the label agreement or the pass time of the first run's adapters.
  agree.py and latency.py were not run on them.
- Nothing about the weight being right.  Its jackknife standard error is 1.685586 ms
  per poll and every score above moves with it.
