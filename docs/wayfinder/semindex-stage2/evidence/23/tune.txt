6  WHAT THIS RUN DOES NOT SHOW
========================================================================
- Nothing about which candidate is better.  Every number here is on seeds 1 to 80 and
  the choice between a and b is the mean replay score on seeds 81 to 100.
- Nothing about L in a simulation.  No decider ran, no port was built and the
  send-time convention is exercised here only by decider.py's stub self-check.
- Nothing about the agreement the ticket asks for.  That is on seeds 81 to 100; the
  held-out agreement above is on seeds 71 to 80, which tuned nothing but which the
  epoch was chosen on, so it is not a held-out number in the ordinary sense either.
- The model being scored is not exactly the model that was optimised.  The training
  forward is batch 16 under a bf16 autocast and the read-out is batch 1 with no
  autocast, and the LoRA branch casts its input to the adapter's float32 while the
  autocast would run the same matmul in bf16.  So the kept epoch minimises a
  cross-entropy of the read-out arithmetic over weights fitted in another one.  Both
  calls are the ones the plan froze and the measurements were made with; what this
  difference costs is measured nowhere.
- Nothing about the labels being right.  Two independent labellings of the same
  decisions agreed on 0.7577 of them, so a model that matched these labels perfectly
  would match a teacher that disagrees with itself on a quarter of its own work.
- That the loss falls is not a result.  The held-out cross-entropy is what chose the
  epoch and the agreement beside it is read against the baseline printed with it,
  never against 1/3.
- Nothing about the pass time with an adapter attached.  The repeat check makes two
  passes and times neither.
