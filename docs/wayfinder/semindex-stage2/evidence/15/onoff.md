# ev15 onoff: on/off workloads, and rules that move their interval on a timeout

Generated by `onoff.py`, 10800 simulations, 12 workers, wall clock 43 s.

Everything below is **simulated against simulated**. The replay was checked against measurements for the logged workload only (../13/validate.md); for a replaced workload there is no measurement to compare with, so these numbers say which testbed runs are worth doing and nothing more.

## The workloads

| workload | records per run | what it is |
|---|---|---|
| W0 | 240 | control: the logged creation times, untouched |
| WO | 164..232, mean 188.9 | on/off regular: first ON at U[13, 33] s, then ON 20 s / OFF 20 s until 110 s |

Each workload is a function of (p, seed, origin) through `random.Random(f"ev15:{name}:{p}:{seed}:{origin}")`, so every rule sees the same creation times on one (p, seed) and the two origins are independent. While ON an origin creates 4 records every 2 s, the testbed's rate and batch size. All times lie in [t0+13 s, t0+110 s]. The outages, agent_start, t0 and the topology are the donor run's own and are not replaced.

T37 is evidence/13's rule: on a timeout its interval is unchanged. T37r and T37b differ from it in that one step only, and they are the reason this directory has its own engine subclass (`Engine.resolve` in `onoff.py`). The subclass sets the interval and leaves everything after it -- the jitter draw, `applied`, `max(sent + applied, now)` and the re-arm -- to ../13/replay.py, and it skips a resolution that does not belong to the neighbor's newest outstanding poll, which is where the simulator resolves a timeout today.

## Headline gain: early records only

The headline of this step is scored on the records created at or before t0+72 s, as ../14/after.py does, because evidence/14 found that a record created late is censored more often under a sparse rule and that spec.md's cens flag cannot catch it (the flag only fires when the RULE is more censored than its bracketing arms). Polls are counted over the whole window. `gain = 1 - delay(rule) / delay_frontier(polls_sent(rule))`, the frontier being piecewise-linear log(delay) over log(polls_sent) through the seven simulated fixed arms of the same cell and workload. 95 % intervals: 2000-resample bootstrap over the 20 seeds, `default_rng(20261117)`, frontier and rule recomputed from the same resampled seeds.

## Assumptions and deviations from spec.md

1. **An ON block is half-open.** spec.md says "ON 20 s, OFF 20 s" and "while ON an origin creates 4 records every 2 s" without saying whether the instant where ON ends still carries a tick. It is read as [b, b+L), so a 20 s ON block has 10 ticks, the last 18 s into the block, and the instant ON ends is the first OFF instant. With the other reading each WO block would hold 11 ticks.
2. **Records per run vary by seed under WO and WV**, because the first ON instant and (under WV) every block length are drawn per seed. evidence/14's workloads all had one count per cell and its script asserted so; that assertion is dropped here and the count is reported as a mean with its range. The `records` column of the csv files holds the integer where every seed agrees (W0) and the mean otherwise, with `records_min` and `records_max` beside it. `polls after complete` uses each run's own record count, so it still means "polls sent once the node held everything".
3. **WV's block lengths are drawn in order, ON first**, from `expovariate(1/20)` clipped to [4, 60] s, alternating ON and OFF until the generation window is past. The first ON start is drawn before them, from the same per-origin stream.
4. **The headline is the early-record scoring**, as spec.md fixes it, and the all-record gain is printed beside it. Both use the same seven arms, the same resample matrix and the same code (../13/trickle.py's `gain_save`, `boot_values` and `ci` applied to a four-column early matrix padded to seven), rather than ../14/after.py's separate scalar interpolation. ../14/after.py's `early_score` itself is imported unchanged, so the early numbers come from the scorer that was written independently of the main one.
5. **The paired difference uses a plain `> 0` on its lower bound**, not the ZERO band spec.md's gain reading uses: in the no-outage cell the difference is exactly zero in every resample, so the two readings agree there, and spec.md's 1e-9 rule is written for the gain, not for the difference. One counted case does sit near the threshold (see the line under the inference 2 table), so the ZERO band would not have changed the count but a thinner threshold would. The ZERO band IS applied to inference 1's gain bound, as evidence/14 applies it.
6. **A resample where either rule falls outside the arms' range yields no difference.** The interval then rests on the resamples where both are inside, and the `boot_n_diff` column says how many those were. spec.md does not say what such a resample does.
7. **No gain is suppressed for a non-monotone frontier.** spec.md asks for no suppression in this step (evidence/14 suppressed one case, under WR only); the `frontier_monotone` and `all_frontier_monotone` columns carry the fact instead.
8. **The new rule key is `on_timeout`** with the values "reset" and "backoff". A rule dict without the key is evidence/13's rule, bit for bit: that is what the W0 check measures.
9. **The tick-lag stream seed is `trickle:{donor}` for every workload**, the literal string of ../13/trickle.py, which is what makes the W0 control reproduce evidence/14. Only the seed is shared between rules; they draw different numbers of lags per poll, so the values diverge from the first interval change on.
10. **The bootstrap's unit is the seed, each seed's value being the mean over its 5 replicates**, while the point estimates pool all 100 (seed, replicate) runs. This is ../13/trickle.py's deviation 2, inherited with its code: for sums the two orders agree exactly, for the ratios they differ in the last digits.
11. **Bytes use the same `REP_B[p]` for every rule**, as evidence/13 and /14 do: the mean reply size is taken not to depend on the rule, and on a replaced workload not on the workload either, which is a second assumption nothing here checks. The headline is stated in polls_sent, not in bytes.
12. **No tuning.** The workloads, arms and rules listed in spec.md are the only ones run, and every number is reported as it came out.
13. **What a replaced workload cannot carry over is validation.** ../13/validate.md measured the replay against the logged workload; an on/off workload may exercise the simulator where no measurement has ever checked it. The three I_min 3.695 s rules stay above the 2 s interest lifetime, so unlike evidence/13's sub-2 s variants they do not depend on source branches no logged run reaches; what is new and unmeasured here is the timeout step itself.
14. **The csv files carry more than spec.md asks for**, so that a reader does not have to rerun the script to see the alternative: `save` (polls saved at equal delay) with its interval for both scorings, and the paired differences on the all-record scoring as well as on the headline one. The readings above use only what spec.md names: the headline gain, and the headline paired difference.
15. **What the per-run assertions do and do not cover.** ../14/workload.py's `check_created` is reused unchanged and asserts spec.md's list constraints for every run: both origins are h0 and h4, every time is a whole millisecond in [t0+13 s, t0+110 s], and each origin's seq runs 1..N in non-decreasing time order. That the records come 4 to a tick, 2 s apart while ON, is enforced by the generator's construction and is not separately asserted.
